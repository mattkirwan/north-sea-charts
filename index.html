How far out by how far ahead

The forecast starts from NOAA's wave model (GFS-Wave, WAVEWATCH III): every swell it has in its sea nearest Saltburn, 20 km out in Tees Bay, hour by hour (until Oct 2026 from its point 150 km north-east, where swell running down from the north can be three times the size it is in the bay). Each that can reach the beach is turned into breaking size here by our own rule: the buoy rule below, scaled down, most for short-period swell, which loses the most height on the way in. That scaling is provisional, from the first reports, and it's what reports will tune. Every run forecasts every hour of the next 5 days, and every prediction is kept, so each hour ends up with around 20, made from five days ahead down to the day itself. A report saves all of them for its time, and "How far out" compares them with what you saw and with the Whitby buoy, by how far ahead they were made.

The long-period watch is NOAA's own wave model (GFS-Wave, WAVEWATCH III) at points on the way in, from Jan Mayen and west of the Hebrides down to 150 km off Saltburn: every swell it sees there, hour by hour, collected every half hour.

The estimate now is breaking height from the nearest buoy's height and period (Komar and Gaughan), less for swell arriving at an angle to Saltburn's beach. The buoy measures everything at once, so only the swell's share of its height counts, worked out from its mean period (the middle of its last three readings): a clean swell's mean period is about three quarters of its peak period, and local chop pulls it down towards 4 s. Each report also keeps the readings and this estimate from the moment it was made.
